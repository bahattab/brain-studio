# Reference-derived product requirements

The supplied reference collection contains personal reports, sample reports, assessment brochures, training material, and questionnaire forms. Only generic product capabilities are specified here. The source inventory, exact document/page references, extraction results, archive defects, and detailed review evidence remain in a separate private audit and are excluded from public delivery.

## Feature requirements and boundaries

| ID | Observed capability | Requirement for the independent application | Scoring and interpretation boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| RF01 | Individual reports with multiple assessment domains | Show instrument identity, version, completion date, language, domain scores, scale endpoints, and concise explanatory sections. | Distinguish raw scores, item means, descriptive ranges, and validated norms. Do not silently turn a mean into a population percentile. |
| RF02 | Behavioral and personality dimension summaries | Provide research-attributed dimensions, readable profiles, strengths to explore, and contextual limitations. | New items, translations, shortened forms, and narrative interpretations need explicit adaptation status. They do not inherit a commercial instrument's certification. |
| RF03 | Interest profiles and career-oriented discussion | Display interest dimensions, preferred activities, and questions for exploring possible work environments. | Interests are not competence, employability, predicted success, or a recommendation to reject a candidate. |
| RF04 | Reasoning and skills report sections | Where independently authored or licensed tasks and an answer key exist, report correct responses and clearly defined task-level results. | Do not recreate proprietary reasoning items or claim IQ, aptitude norms, or learning potential from unvalidated tasks. |
| RF05 | Individual-versus-role target range charts | Allow user-defined scenario targets on compatible dimensions and display each dimension's descriptive difference from the chosen target. | Do not copy job-model weights, publish an employment suitability percentage, or describe a target gap as validated prediction. User-created targets must be labeled as such. |
| RF06 | Comparisons across multiple role scenarios | Present a side-by-side scenario table using the same instrument/version and visibly disclose missing dimensions. | This is a planning aid, not a succession recommendation or hiring ranking. Do not hide an overall fit formula behind a familiar report layout. |
| RF07 | Coaching reports | Link selected development themes to original coaching prompts, small experiments, and editable notes. | Report-derived coaching text and automated personality-to-performance predictions are proprietary or unvalidated and must not be copied. |
| RF08 | Interview guides | Offer original, open-ended discussion questions linked to selected themes and a place for examples and notes. | These are reflection prompts, not a validated selection interview or an automatically scored hiring decision. |
| RF09 | Development plans | Record an action, intended outcome, owner, target date, follow-up date, and progress notes; revisit the plan alongside prior reports. | Keep self-reported progress separate from assessment scores. A checklist is not a psychometric improvement measure. |
| RF10 | Person-to-person and supervisor/employee comparisons | Compare compatible dimension scores and discuss differences in communication, structure, independence, and working preferences. | No compatibility, managerial-fit, relationship-success, or retention prediction without appropriate validation. Explain that different scores need not imply conflict. |
| RF11 | Team summary reports | Show consenting participants' compatible dimension distributions and group summaries, with clear membership and missing-data counts. | Use safe aggregation and avoid revealing individual data through small-group reports. Team averages do not diagnose team effectiveness. |
| RF12 | Multi-rater leadership feedback | Separate self, supervisor, peers, and other observer groups; show item/domain means and valid response counts. Include an explicit not-observed response. | Not-observed responses are missing data, not zeros. New feedback items remain a custom development survey rather than a certified commercial leadership assessment. |
| RF13 | Self-versus-observer differences and disagreement | Show transparent descriptive differences, response counts, and dispersion when sufficient responses exist. | Do not import commercial favorable zones, gap thresholds, talent classifications, or reference populations. Do not imply precision unsupported by the number of raters. |
| RF14 | Importance alignment | Let participants identify priorities and compare their overlap with an explicitly stated calculation. | Agreement on priorities is not evidence of competence or predictive validity. Do not reuse a proprietary scoring rule without a licensed specification. |
| RF15 | Sales and service development reports | Provide optional custom skill-observation themes and original prompts for customer interactions, prospecting, needs discovery, follow-up, and service recovery. | Distinguish a custom checklist from validated sales aptitude, customer-service suitability, or performance prediction. |
| RF16 | Work behavior, motivation, stress, and change sections | Offer contextual self-reflection and observer notes where a supported instrument or clearly labeled custom survey supplies the data. | Avoid clinical diagnoses and inferred stress vulnerability. Do not equate preference with ability or fixed behavior. |
| RF17 | Topic-level and difficulty-level test breakdowns | For tasks with a defensible answer key, report attempted counts, correct counts, elapsed time where actually measured, and subject/topic/difficulty breakdowns. | Define partial credit and denominators before reporting percentages. A task score is not a normed proficiency category unless the standard-setting evidence exists. |
| RF18 | Concise summaries and full reports | Provide a concise summary plus accessible detailed results, comparisons, notes, and development actions. Export only data selected by the user. | Include source attribution, instrument/version, scoring method, missing-data policy, language/adaptation status, and limitations in exports. |
| RF19 | Behavioral-style and leadership training resources | Explain that multiple conceptual frameworks exist; use properly attributed and authorized material where available. | Familiar commercial style labels and diagrams do not provide an authorized item bank, scoring key, norms, or certification. Do not claim an independent survey is that branded assessment. |
| RF20 | Historical and localized report variants | Preserve stable item IDs, unchanged scoring across language switches, report date conventions, and RTL/LTR readability. | Linguistic translation does not establish measurement equivalence. Describe generated translations and any review or validation that has actually occurred. |

## Evidence classification

An observed capability means that the private reference collection contains that type of report or an explicit description of the capability. The application requirement is an independent design decision; it does not imply that the original product's algorithm has been recovered. Private evidence records differentiate exact text extraction, local OCR, non-paginated text recovery, and feature inference. Scanned figures, commercial norms, proprietary weights, and psychometric claims retain their respective limitations.

## Publication and data protection

Public source, screenshots, fixtures, documentation, and releases must contain only newly authored content, licensed or appropriately attributed research resources, and synthetic participants. Private report files, extracted text, participant identities, personal scores, source paths, report screenshots, and private audit artifacts must remain excluded. The application must disclose where local profiles are stored, provide deliberate exports and deletion, and avoid transmitting report or participant data during analysis.
